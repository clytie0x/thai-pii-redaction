# Thai PII research advances: model alternatives and bounded experiments

Research snapshot: 2026-10-31. Primary sources only. This was a read-only literature/model-metadata investigation; no model weights were downloaded, no packages installed, no inference or training run, and no external held-out directory or blind corpus opened. The assigned machine constraint is 24 logical CPUs and 32 GB RAM, with no observed GPU. Resource estimates below distinguish file metadata, arithmetic, vendor measurements, and unmeasured local behavior.

## Which alternatives have evidence of Thai capability?

### Takeaway
ThaiNER and Thai-specific pretrained encoders have actual Thai evaluation evidence. PII Engineer explicitly includes Thai but currently provides much weaker reproducibility evidence; popular GLiNER PII variants must not be described as Thai-validated simply because they say multilingual.

### Cited Findings
- **ThaiNER CRF 1.5.1, dated 2021-06-21:** the official card identifies a CRF with CC0 licensing, 5,089 training sentences and 1,274 test sentences, and explicitly limits intended use to Thai news-domain NER. Its token/BIO table reports PERSON beginning-token recall 0.78 and LOCATION beginning-token recall 0.70; these are not end-to-end PII span or masking measurements. Thus CRF weaknesses in address/name coverage are plausible but must be measured in this project. [PyThaiNLP model card](https://pythainlp.org/Model-Cards/NER/)
- **WangchanBERTa, original paper 2021-01-24:** trained on 78.5 GB of Thai text; the paper evaluates sequence and token classification, including CRF, XLM-R and mBERT comparisons. Its official base card lists ThaiNER and LST20 token-classification checkpoints, so a Thai transformer comparator already exists rather than requiring an entirely new architecture. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09635); [base model card](https://huggingface.co/airesearch/wangchanberta-base-att-spm-uncased)
- **ThaiNER v2 ready-made checkpoint:** `pythainlp/thainer-corpus-v2-base-model` fine-tunes WangchanBERTa; developer-reported test precision/recall/F1 are 0.8199/0.8781/0.8480. The card warns that generic token-classification inference gives wrong Thai tags and supplies custom preprocessing/reconstruction. Its license metadata is CC-BY-4.0. This is genuine Thai NER evidence, not proof of this project's raw-character redaction correctness. [Official checkpoint card](https://huggingface.co/pythainlp/thainer-corpus-v2-base-model)
- **PhayaThaiBERT, original 2023-11-21, v2 2023-12-28:** expands WangchanBERTa vocabulary using non-Thai XLM-R vocabulary to handle unassimilated loanwords/code-switching. The paper grows vocabulary from 25,005 to 249,262 and model size from 106M to 278M. In its controlled fine-tuning comparison, ThaiNER micro/macro F1 is 86.42/74.77 versus WangchanBERTa 84.64/68.19; LST20 NER gains are tiny or mixed, 78.11/72.69 versus 77.99/72.86. Authors explicitly fail to reproduce some original WangchanBERTa results. Therefore cite the paired comparison within this paper, not scores across different papers. These are NER results, not Thai PII masking F2. [Original paper v2, Table 3 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12475v2)
- **PII Engineer Multi NER v2.1:** official card explicitly includes Thai, mDeBERTa-v3-base/GLiNER2, nine named PII labels, ONNX and Apache-2.0. It reports Thai F1 0.885. However the same card advertises 30+ labels/50+ languages, while listing nine labels and 14 language metadata tags. Treat this as a testable candidate, not an established Thai baseline. [Official model card](https://huggingface.co/pii-engineer/PII-Engineer-Multi-NER-v2.1); [machine-readable metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/pii-engineer/PII-Engineer-Multi-NER-v2.1)
- **Fastino GLiNER2-PII, paper 2026-05-11:** seven supported training languages are English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch. Its card explicitly states that non-European locales/scripts have not been measured. Apache-2.0 and 42 label types make it reusable, but it is not a validated Thai competitor. [Official model card, Training Details and Limitations](https://huggingface.co/fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi)
- **Urchade multilingual PII:** `urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1` fine-tunes the multilingual GLiNER backbone on synthetic PII. Its language metadata lists English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese and Italian, with Apache-2.0. Thai is absent. [Official raw model card](https://huggingface.co/urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1/raw/main/README.md)
- **NVIDIA GLiNER-PII, released 2025-10-28:** English-tagged 570M model, 55+ categories, downloadable under NVIDIA Open Model License. The card supports x86 CPU but lists Linux as preferred/supported OS and reports testing on A100. Its strict F1 figures are on Argilla/AI4Privacy/Nemotron datasets; none supplies a Thai-specific result here. [Official model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/gliner-PII)
- **OpenAI Privacy Filter:** official card identifies primarily English training and warns about non-English/non-Latin degradation; downloadable Apache-2.0 weights are 1.5B total/50M active parameters with eight privacy categories. It is relevant as a different decoding approach, not current Thai superiority evidence. The official repository explicitly supports CPU and constrained BIOES/Viterbi decoding. [Official model card](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter); [first-party source repository](https://github.com/openai/privacy-filter)

### Inferences
- A generic switch from CRF to transformer would not be a substantial contribution here: the repository already has `FinetunedEngine`, raw tokenizer offsets, overlapping windows, calibrated label thresholds, synthetic hard negatives/counterfactual pairs, and a ThaiNER-v2 fine-tuning lane. Local sources inspected: `pii_redactor/detectors/finetuned_engine.py`, `training/README.md`, and `docs/research/yrss-accuracy-plan.md`.
- A narrowly justified PhayaThaiBERT comparison would concern English/Thai mixed identifiers and names, with tokenizer fragmentation and boundary errors measured. The paper does not justify replacing the current model across all categories.
- A Thai-first contribution can concern reliable source-span reconstruction and masking utility at CPU budgets, without claiming a new neural architecture or globally novel sequence decoder.

### Gaps
- No independently reproduced Thai PII masking result was found for PII Engineer, Fastino GLiNER2-PII, Urchade PII, NVIDIA PII or OpenAI Privacy Filter in this bounded primary-source search. Absence of a found source is not proof that none exists.
- WangchanBERTa and PhayaThaiBERT base cards inspected did not declare a weight license. Public downloadability is not a license grant; confirm the exact checkpoint and inherited terms before redistribution. The PhayaThaiBERT paper's CC-BY paper license does not license the weights. [WangchanBERTa files](https://huggingface.co/airesearch/wangchanberta-base-att-spm-uncased/tree/main); [PhayaThaiBERT metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/clicknext/phayathaibert)
- Model-card updates have no trustworthy publication date in rendered pages. Where dates matter, use paper version date and repository SHA rather than the search engine's relative age.

## What do resource costs and benchmark caveats mean for local feasibility?

### Takeaway
Inference for one modest encoder at a time is a reasonable local pilot on 32 GB RAM, but actual latency and peak RAM remain unmeasured. Model files, active parameter counts, CPU throughput, full-pipeline overhead and training cost are different quantities.

### Cited Findings
- Read-only Hugging Face API metadata, observed 2026-10-31, gives WangchanBERTa 105,861,549 F32 parameters and a 423,470,574-byte safetensors file; PhayaThaiBERT has 277,724,590 F32 parameters and a 1,110,926,888-byte file. This supports roughly 0.42 GB versus 1.11 GB for stored FP32 weights, not total resident memory. [WangchanBERTa model API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/airesearch/wangchanberta-base-att-spm-uncased); [file API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/airesearch/wangchanberta-base-att-spm-uncased/tree/main?recursive=true); [PhayaThaiBERT model API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/clicknext/phayathaibert); [file API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/clicknext/phayathaibert/tree/main?recursive=true)
- Fastino's prose says 205M, its paper says approximately 0.3B, and actual safetensors metadata reports 307,098,645 F32 parameters with a 1,228,421,964-byte file. Pin the real checkpoint, not the inconsistent marketing size. Observed SHA: `1cb4166094dc58fa8d836429f060d6c95f62b495`. [Model API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi); [file API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi/tree/main?recursive=true)
- PII Engineer offers five ONNX components plus alternative FP32/INT8 encoders. File metadata: INT8 encoder 535,410,041 bytes; FP32 encoder 1,111,250,493; classifier 4,731,923; count embedding 42,669,056; count predictor 4,848,553; span representation 66,124,778. Arithmetic gives 653,784,351 bytes for INT8 plus four heads, versus 1,229,624,803 for FP32 plus heads. Downloading the whole repository also includes both encoders. Observed SHA: `efacd9242671d0300da535207d7952dc3f82f57e`. [File metadata API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/pii-engineer/PII-Engineer-Multi-NER-v2.1/tree/main?recursive=true)
- PII Engineer developer benchmark claims p50 180 ms, p99 400 ms and 700 MB RAM on four AMD cloud vCPUs for approximately 50-word inputs. It mixes a 500-example overall comparison with a 1,200-example language evaluation and identifies v1.3 plus eight postprocessing stages, raising raw F1 0.779 to 0.902. Thai 0.885 is therefore not a clean evaluation of the v2.1 neural weights alone. The page does not provide enough artifacts to reproduce these exact results. [Developer benchmark and methodology](https://pii.engineer/benchmarks)
- Fastino's original paper uses SPY legal/medical exact-span metrics, reports mean F1 0.471, and says its synthetic training annotations were not human validated. The current model card instead reports 0.477 with different baseline numbers and fewer displayed systems. Do not combine these versions or transfer them to Thai; both are developer-run comparisons on another benchmark. [Paper v1, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09973v1); [current model card](https://huggingface.co/fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi)

### Inferences
- PII Engineer is the most practical additional Thai-advertising external candidate because ready-made INT8 ONNX files avoid a first CPU training campaign. Its value must be separated into raw model, its postprocessing and AI Guard's own postprocessing; otherwise any measured gain has no interpretable cause.
- PhayaThaiBERT has about 2.6 times WangchanBERTa's parameter count, largely from vocabulary expansion. This does not establish a 2.6-times latency penalty: input token count, embedding access, ONNX optimization and thread settings affect CPU cost.
- A 1.5B sparse model does not become a 50M-sized memory allocation merely because 50M parameters are active per token. Privacy Filter may fit this RAM budget, but it deserves an actual local pilot before any runtime claim. A 128k advertised context is not an invitation to allocate that input during a first CPU test.
- Avoid full pretraining or an open-ended CPU fine-tuning sweep. The current lane already exposes a 100-step CPU pilot. Any later training comparison should have a declared wall-clock limit and report unfinished/convergence status honestly.

### Gaps
- No local model loading, warm-up, throughput, peak-RSS, tokenizer, ONNX operator, Windows compatibility or quantization-parity test was run. The 24 logical CPUs are not equivalent to the vendor's four cloud vCPUs, and extra threads need not improve latency.
- No consistent independent Thai benchmark, machine-readable PII Engineer predictions, exact Thai sample count, uncertainty interval, entity support, train/test overlap statement or v2.1 evaluation was found.
- CRF runtime and memory must come from AI Guard measurements; the official model card does not establish this machine's numbers.

## Which one or two bounded experiments could justify difficult work?

### Takeaway
Prioritize a decoder/offset experiment on the existing Thai model, then a strictly bounded PII Engineer complementarity pilot. Do not spend the research effort implementing a second generic fine-tuned engine or repeating existing ONNX work.

### Cited Findings
- Privacy Filter's first-party implementation explains constrained BIOES/Viterbi decoding and operating-point calibration as a way to improve coherent boundaries and precision/recall behavior. This is a concrete established technique worth comparing to AI Guard's current token-wise maximum-probability window voting and BIO merge; it is not a claim of algorithmic novelty. [Official model card, Sequence Decoding Rationale](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter)
- PhayaThaiBERT's code-switching motivation gives a mechanistic reason to include mixed-script counterfactuals, rather than randomly adding more model families. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12475)
- PII Engineer provides a downloadable Thai-tagged ONNX checkpoint with distinct model family and nine PII labels; it is a plausible external comparator but its advertised scores are insufficient for adoption. [Official checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/pii-engineer/PII-Engineer-Multi-NER-v2.1)

### Inferences
1. **Existing-model span coherence and Thai boundary robustness.** Reuse the current fine-tuned weights and cached logits. Compare four predefined arms: current independent argmax/BIO merging; legal-transition sequence decoding; threshold-only calibration; decoding plus calibration. Keep the structured checksum/rule layer fixed, and measure both raw neural spans and the full ensemble. Use a newly authored visible synthetic research set with held-out template families: Thai names beside English role labels, repeated identical values, code-switched names, no-space Thai, line breaks, punctuation, explicit non-person uses, and entities crossing token-window seams. Existing raw offset mapping and overlap support remain the baseline, not a new feature claim. If perturbations change offsets, generate authoritative spans from construction, then manually review a stratified slice.
   - **Bound:** no neural retraining initially; one fixed checkpoint, one predefined decoder grid, a small development split and frozen family-disjoint final synthetic split. Cap engineering to the span decoder and harness; leave product defaults untouched during research. No access to external held-out data.
   - **Evidence:** paired character recall/F2, exact typed-span recall/F1, false-positive characters on O-only texts, per-family results, paired confidence intervals resampled by template family, end-to-end p50/p95 latency and peak RSS. Record checkpoint/source/data hashes and all operating parameters.
   - **Worthwhile outcome:** establish whether coverage losses are decoder/alignment failures that can be reduced without new model weights. It is a credible Thai-specific empirical contribution if gains survive unseen synthetic families and utility controls; it is not a new global NER architecture. Stop if the gain vanishes under matched thresholds or comes only from expanding every span.
2. **Thai PII Engineer complementarity with a CPU budget.** In a separately authorized future pilot, pin the v2.1 ONNX SHA and run only its INT8 encoder plus the four heads. First validate source offsets on repeated values, Thai combining marks, tabs/newlines and mixed-script strings. Compare current CRF+rules, current fine-tuned+rules, raw PII Engineer mapped to the common schema, and one explicitly defined union/cascade. Report an oracle union to bound possible complementarity before implementing fusion. Do not assume generic model-loading examples work: the published artifact is split ONNX, and its own Rust runtime may be needed.
   - **Bound:** approximately 300-500 freshly fabricated Thai snippets across names, addresses, dates, structured IDs and hard negatives, separated by generation/template family. Start inference-only, one checkpoint, 1/4/8-thread timing pilot, and an owner-chosen latency/RAM ceiling. Calibration stays on development families; no private upload, paid API, cloud deployment or external blind scoring.
   - **Evidence:** common-label boundary metrics and character masking F2; error-overlap matrix with bootstrap intervals; raw-model versus postprocessor ablation; INT8 parity on a fixed small synthetic subset if FP32 comparison is later authorized. Log only synthetic text and metadata.
   - **Worthwhile outcome:** determine whether a model trained for Southeast Asian PII recovers a meaningful class of errors the Thai news-NER lane cannot. Proceed to a fusion experiment only if there are repeatable complementary true positives within the negative-text and resource budget. Otherwise the rigorous negative result rules out an attractive but unsupported replacement.

### Gaps
- These are proposed experiments, not completed results or promises of improvement. Acceptance margins and a useful latency ceiling must be selected before inspecting candidate results.
- A PhayaThaiBERT swap is a secondary experiment only if mixed-script failures dominate the measured inventory, its exact weight license is resolved, and the first two experiments fail to answer the question. Its general NER paper does not by itself justify a costly CPU fine-tuning campaign.
- Local synthetic gains support a claim about controlled Thai mechanisms. Human-annotated, family-independent and genuinely fresh external evaluation would still be needed before claiming general Thai PII accuracy or production superiority.
