# PDPA coverage and lifecycle: legal scope

Research cutoff: 2026-10-31. Research and design requirements only. No application changes, deployment, certification, real personal data, or external held-out data were used. Thai statutory text controls; English labels below are working translations. The 2019 Act was checked in Thai and against the ETDA-hosted unofficial English translation. The 2024 erasure and 2026 access notifications were rendered and visually checked because their extracted Thai text has a broken character map.

## What data must the coverage model represent?

### Takeaway

Section 6 defines personal data through direct or indirect identifiability, rather than an exhaustive list of identifier types. Section 26 adds a separate, enumerated sensitive-data layer and a notification-dependent catchall. A detector taxonomy can support these concepts, but cannot establish that every personal datum has been found or that a processing activity is lawful. [Act, sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)

### Cited Findings

- Section 6 covers information relating to a person enabling identification directly or indirectly; it excludes information specifically about deceased persons. Section 4 exceptions and section 5 territorial scope are separate questions from data classification. Do not turn an applicability exception into a claim that the text contains no personal information. [Act, sections 4–6](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 26 expressly names race, ethnic origin, political opinions, cult/religious/philosophical beliefs, sexual behavior, criminal records, health, disability, trade-union information, genetic data, biometric data, and other similarly affecting information **as prescribed by the Committee**. The catchall is not permission for software to invent new statutory categories. [Act, section 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 26 defines biometric data as personal data arising from techniques or technology concerning a person's physical or behavioral characteristics that can distinguish that person from others, with facial-recognition, iris-recognition, and fingerprint-recognition examples. An ordinary photo or voice recording may be identifying personal data under section 6; the fact that it depicts a face or contains speech does not by itself establish this section 26 biometric definition. [Act, section 26 paragraphs 2–3](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- The 2024 erasure notification lists identifiable facial images separately from biometric data among direct identifiers, and also requires addressing indirect identifiers such as birth date, age, position, affiliation, service dates, residence/workplace, and IP address. This supports a broader identifiability inventory than named entity recognition alone. [PDPC erasure notification B.E. 2567, clause 6](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf)

### Inferences

The following is a **proposed coverage matrix**, not a claim that these labels already exist in code or that examples have a binding legal interpretation. Every section 26 row requires personal attribution and section 6 identifiability; ambiguous linkage stays reviewable. Statutory anchors: [sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf).

| Coverage concept | Proposed representation / synthetic example | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Direct identifiers | Person name, national/passport/tax/social-security ID, customer/employee/account identifier, personal phone/email, identifiable face, username | Validate identifiers where possible, but invalid format/checksum does not prove non-personal data. Preserve source spans. |
| Indirect identifiers and linkage | Birth date, age, location, role, small workplace, rare event, device/IP/account linkage, relationship, combinations across sentences | Assess the combination and external context; absence of a name does not establish anonymity. |
| Race, เชื้อชาติ | Separate `racial_origin` signal | Do not infer race solely from surname, appearance, citizenship, or nationality words. |
| Ethnic origin, เผ่าพันธุ์ | Separate `ethnic_origin` signal, for example a person's explicitly stated ethnic group | Keep distinct from race and nationality even if the UI groups origin-related findings. |
| Political opinions | An attributable statement of a person's political opinion | A party name, election headline, policy discussion, or workplace topic alone does not establish that person's opinion. |
| Cult or doctrinal beliefs, ความเชื่อในลัทธิ | Explicitly attributed personal belief | Keep this scope visible rather than implementing only a religion dictionary. |
| Religious beliefs, ศาสนา | Attributed personal religion or belief | A temple address, holiday, school name, or historical discussion alone is insufficient. |
| Philosophical beliefs, ปรัชญา | Attributed personal philosophical belief | A quotation or discussion of a philosopher is not automatically the speaker's belief. |
| Sexual behavior, พฤติกรรมทางเพศ | Attributed information about sexual behavior | Do not silently rename the statutory category to all sex/gender words; retain uncertainty about adjacent concepts. |
| Criminal records | Attributed criminal-record information, with provenance/status | Distinguish allegation, arrest, prosecution, judgment, acquittal and absence of a record; do not convert any crime word into a conviction. |
| Health data | Diagnosis, treatment, test results, symptoms or attributed health status | Negative findings can still be personal health information. A general medical explanation is not automatically personal. |
| Disability | Attributed disability information | Keep separate coverage even where health also applies; a general accessibility discussion is not enough. |
| Trade-union information | Attributed union membership or union-related personal information | Union names in general news do not by themselves attribute membership to a person. |
| Genetic data | Attributed genetic test/profile/sequence information | Genetic text linked to an identifiable person needs its own route; not every DNA topic is personal, and ordinary family relationships are not automatically genetic-test data. |
| Biometric data | Identification-capable face/iris/fingerprint/voice template or explicitly described recognition processing | Separate biometric identification from ordinary photo/audio and mere mentions of recognition technology. Text can signal a biometric record; actual image/audio/template handling needs separate capability evidence. |
| Additional Committee-prescribed categories | Versioned category registry with notification URL, scope, effective date, reviewer and change history | Do not populate as statutory sensitive data without an applicable verified notification. Local risk tags can be broader but must say they are policy tags. |

Functional requirements inferred from the matrix:

1. Keep `personal_data_candidate`, `sensitive_category_candidate`, `attributed_subject`, `attribution_status`, `polarity`, `temporality`, `source_span`, `provenance`, `confidence`, and `review_status` separate. Topic detection is weaker evidence than an attributed personal statement. This follows the distinction between information relating to an identifiable person and the categories of that information. [Act, sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
2. Treat negation as a modifier, not a universal exclusion. Synthetic `คุณสมชายไม่มีเชื้อเอชไอวี` attributes a negative health result to a named person; `เอชไอวีไม่ใช่แบคทีเรีย` is a general proposition. The first remains a personal-health candidate. The examples are design interpretations, not regulator rulings. [Act, sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
3. Preserve `nationality` as a potentially personal general attribute; require explicit origin context before adding race/ethnicity. The named statutory list does not say that nationality always equals race. [Act, sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
4. Return `unknown / needs contextual review` for ambiguous referents, quotations, fictional examples, reported speech, inferred attributes, and attribution across documents. Do not advertise semantic certainty from a keyword hit. [Act, sections 6 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
5. Keep sensitive classification separate from product action. A section 26 warning does not decide blocking, redaction, legal basis, necessity, or the lawfulness of a later use. [Act, sections 19, 21–22, 24, 26–27](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)

### Gaps

- **Not verified:** whether a Committee notification currently adds categories under section 26's catchall. Searches of PDPC/MDES/Gazette-related sources did not establish one. PDPC's main website returned 403, and a search result's absence is not proof that no notification exists. Preserve the catchall extension point and require an authoritative legal inventory check before claiming the named list is exhaustive as of the cutoff.
- The Act does not provide an exhaustive machine-readable dictionary of identifying combinations or a deterministic test for all natural-language attribution cases. Coverage of new domains and modalities needs labelled evaluation and human review. No model-accuracy claim is made here.

## How should lawful basis, consent and capacity be designed?

### Takeaway

Consent is one route, not a requirement for every processing activity. Store a purpose-specific legal decision first, then execute consent or the documented exception and its safeguards. The system can track evidence and enforce an approved rule; an authorized person must decide whether the legal condition actually fits. [Act, sections 19–27](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)

### Cited Findings

- Section 19 requires distinguishable, accessible, understandable consent requests that specify purposes and respect free choice; unrelated processing cannot be made a condition of entering a contract or obtaining a service. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent, subject to the statutory qualifications, and does not undo earlier lawful processing. Explain consequential effects of withdrawal. [Act, section 19](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- PDPC guidance requires specific purposes rather than bundled general purposes, a clear affirmative act, understandable notice, and easy withdrawal. It describes evidence and proportionate verification, including for minors and representatives. This is official guidance hosted by MDES, not a substitute for the Act. [PDPC consent guidance, sections 3–7](https://www.mdes.go.th/uploads/tinymce/source/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%AA/%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%B3%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%83%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%82%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%A1.pdf)
- Section 21 limits processing to notified purposes unless the new purpose is notified with prior consent, or the Act/other law permits it. Section 22 requires collection only as necessary for lawful purposes. Section 23 notice includes purposes, mandatory/optional provision and consequences, categories, retention period or expected standard, recipients, controller/contact details and rights. [Act, sections 21–23](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 24 exceptions cover safeguarded public-interest historical/archive/research/statistical purposes; preventing danger to life/body/health; necessary contract performance or requested pre-contract steps; public task/official authority; legitimate interests subject to the data subject's fundamental rights; and legal obligations. A generic `legitimate_interest=true` does not establish the required balance. [Act, section 24](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 26 permits explicit consent or its own exceptions: vital protection when the subject cannot consent; specified safeguarded nonprofit activities without outside disclosure; information made public with the subject's explicit consent; legal claims; and necessary law-based specified medical/care, public-health, employment/social-protection, safeguarded research/statistics/public-interest, or substantial-public-interest conditions. These contain limits, necessity and safeguards; an ordinary section 24 contract basis alone does not resolve the section 26 question. Criminal records have an additional authority-control or Committee-prescribed protection requirement. [Act, section 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 27 also governs use/disclosure and requires recording relevant non-consent uses/disclosures under section 39. Being publicly accessible is not a blanket permission to reuse sensitive information: section 26(3) refers to public disclosure with explicit consent. [Act, sections 26(3), 27 and 39](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- **Capacity boundary:** Thai section 20(2) says age **not exceeding ten**, therefore `age <= 10`, unlike the ETDA unofficial English wording “below ... ten”. For older minors, determine whether they can act alone under the referenced Civil and Commercial Code provisions; otherwise parental-authority consent is also needed. The section includes marriage/legal capacity exceptions and separately requires an authorized custodian for incompetence or curator for quasi-incompetence; it also applies to withdrawal, notices, rights and complaints. [Thai Act, section 20, PDF page 10](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf); [ETDA unofficial translation, PDF page 8, translation discrepancy](https://www.etda.or.th/getattachment/e820df2c-848f-4e03-86cb-a9dad38cc713/ENG-Version.aspx)

### Inferences

Proposed processing record, linked to the section 23 notice and section 39 record, should include `activity_id`, purpose/version, controller, categories, subject groups, collection source, operations, recipients, necessity assessment, section 24 route, section 26 consent/exception where relevant, safeguard references, capacity decision, retention policy, transfers, and human approver. This is a design, not a verbatim mandatory database schema. [Act, sections 20–27 and 39–40](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)

Proposed consent states: `not_requested -> requested -> granted | declined`; `granted -> withdrawn | invalidated`; use `expired` only where an approved purpose or explicit time limit requires it. Store an append-only change history with purpose/category/recipient scope, notice and consent-text versions or hashes, language/channel, affirmative action, timestamp, actor/representative authority, minimal verification evidence reference, withdrawal event and effective propagation state. Do not replace evidence with a single editable boolean. [Act, sections 19–23](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf); [PDPC consent guidance, sections 3–7](https://www.mdes.go.th/uploads/tinymce/source/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%AA/%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%B3%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%83%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%82%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%A1.pdf)

- Before each approved purpose runs, check scope and current consent where consent is its route. On withdrawal, stop that consent-dependent purpose and propagate revocation to pending jobs/recipients; separately review any continuing obligation/other lawful purpose. Do not silently change the legal basis to defeat withdrawal. [Act, sections 19, 21, 24, 26–27](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Keep a capacity state such as `adult_or_legally_capable`, `minor_can_act_alone`, `guardian_required`, `custodian_required`, `curator_required`, or `undetermined`. Age alone must not approve a contract, decide guardianship, or infer incapacity from a disability signal. [Act, sections 20 and 26](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Automate receipt/versioning, scope checks, reminders and propagation acknowledgments. Humans decide applicability, legal basis, necessity, balancing, the exact sensitive-data exception, lawful representative authority, and whether a purpose change requires new consent. [Act, sections 19–27](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)

### Gaps

- The usual civil majority threshold is twenty, but this research did not verify a current consolidated primary Civil and Commercial Code text. Do not present `under 20 => always guardian consent` as PDPA logic; section 20 and the incorporated capacity provisions require a specific decision. Do not copy old marriage-age values from historical guides.
- No particular customer activity, research protocol, legitimate-interest assessment, sector-specific retention law, or criminal-record safeguard notification was adjudicated. Those decisions remain outside detector output and must be documented by the responsible controller/legal reviewer.

## How should retention, rights, records, processors and transfers work?

### Takeaway

A purpose-aware lifecycle and request workflow is required alongside data detection. In particular, the currently verified access notification has a 30-day response rule with a conditional extension, while the verified erasure notification uses 90 days. Neither supports a universal “all rights within 30 days” claim. [Access notification B.E. 2569, clauses 7–12](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf); [erasure notification B.E. 2567, clauses 3–9](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf)

### Cited Findings

| Right / duty | Source-mapped rule | Workflow consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Access, copy, acquisition source | Section 30; 2026 notification published 16 July 2026, effective after 60 days. Verify request details and identity/authority without delay and within 15 days of receipt. If incomplete, notify required corrections and allow at least 10 days; a corrected complete request is treated as received when complete. Fulfil without delay and within 30 days; extensive data/other necessity can permit at most a further 30 days with reasons/details notified. [Notification clauses 2, 6–7, 10](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf) | Track original receipt, verification due, deficiency notice, complete receipt, response due, approved extension and notice separately. Do not silently reset clocks or presume every identity check pauses them. |
| Access refusal / partial disclosure | Law/court orders or others' rights/freedoms; the notification addresses clearly insubstantial/unreasonably burdensome requests and requires providing as much as possible without undue harm, for example by removing others' information, and recording refusals/reasons. [Notification clause 8](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf) | Human decision with redaction review, reasons and request history; do not use “contains another person's data” to reject the entire request automatically. |
| Access channels / evidence | In-person and post channels must be available; electronic/other channels may be added. Requests, supporting documents and fulfilment/refusal details must be retained for at least two years. Fee rules are specific and conditional, not a general paywall. [Notification clauses 5–6, 11–12](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf) | A web form alone does not cover required channels. Put mandatory request evidence in a protected, scheduled case store; a minimized application log is not the complete case record. |
| Portability | Automatic/readable/common formats and applicable consent/contract or Committee-prescribed bases; technical feasibility and public-task/legal-duty/others'-rights limits apply. [Act, section 31](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf) | Validate scope, receiving destination and safe format; do not call every PDF export portability. No standalone numeric deadline established here. |
| Objection | Public-task/legitimate-interest routes with specified override/legal-claim conditions; direct marketing; specified research with public-interest qualification. Clear immediate segregation is required when objection is notified. [Act, section 32](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf) | Apply an immediate use/purpose restriction while legal review resolves the applicable objection; do not wait for an ordinary 30-day queue before suppressing direct marketing. |
| Erasure/destruction/anonymization | Section 33 grounds include no longer necessary, withdrawal without another lawful basis, qualifying objection and unlawful processing, with statutory exceptions. The 2024 notification requires action without delay and within 90 days for qualifying section 33 requests; covers copies and backups; technical overwrite delay has strict safeguards. Unlawful processing under section 33(4) must use deletion/destruction rather than anonymization where clause 7 applies. [Act, section 33](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf); [notification clauses 3–8](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf) | Separate legal exemption from technical inability. Do not equate masking, a retained mapping, or removal from the active database with completed erasure. |
| Restriction | Pending accuracy correction; unlawful data when restriction requested instead of erasure; no longer needed by controller but required for subject's legal claims; pending specified objection assessments. [Act, section 34](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf) | Keep restriction flags and permitted access separate from ordinary active use; no standalone numeric deadline established here. |
| Accuracy / correction | Controller must ensure accurate, current, complete and non-misleading data; refusals to act on correction requests require record/reasons and complaint route. [Act, sections 35–36](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf) | Propagate corrections to indexes/caches/relevant copies while keeping minimized lawful history; no standalone numeric deadline established here. |
| Scheduled retention review | Section 37(3) requires an examination system for deletion/destruction when retention ends or data exceeds necessity, with statutory exceptions. Clause 9 of the 2024 notification applies specified method/safeguard provisions to this duty, not the section 33 request deadline as a universal retention schedule. [Act, section 37(3)](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf); [notification clause 9](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf) | Expiry must trigger work. Do not add a routine 90-day grace period to every expired record. |

- Retention exceptions include specified expression, archive/research/public-task, medical/public-health, legal-claim and legal-compliance purposes under the cited provisions. A legal hold needs an actual ground and bounded review, rather than a permanent `keep_all=true`. [Act, section 33 paragraph 2 and section 37(3)](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- If technical overwrite prevents prompt erasure, the 2024 rule requires organizational/technical and where needed physical measures, no intention to reuse, no service/decision use, prevention of others' access/use/disclosure, appropriate security, and completion without delay when possible. It is not an unrestricted backup exemption. [Erasure notification, clause 4](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf)
- Section 39 records cover collected data, category-specific purposes, controller details, retention, access rights/methods/conditions, specified non-consent use/disclosure, refusals and security measures. Small-organization exceptions have conditions; do not assume a research project or SME is exempt. [Act, section 39](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Section 40 requires processors to follow lawful controller instructions, implement security, notify the controller of breaches, maintain prescribed processing records, and operate under a controller–processor agreement. Going beyond instructions can make a processor a controller for that processing. [Act, section 40](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf)
- Sections 28–29 address cross-border protection separately from collection/use legal basis: adequacy and statutory section 28 exceptions; certified group policies or appropriate safeguards with enforceable rights/effective remedies under section 29. The 2023 implementing notifications exist for both sections. [Act, sections 28–29](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf); [section 28 notification](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/14915.pdf); [government index of section 29 notification, Gazette PDF 14913](https://sciinfo.dss.go.th/products/detail/639/4)

### Inferences

Proposed retention specification, implementing [sections 23, 33, 37(3), 39–40](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf) and [2024 notification clauses 3–9](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf):

- Define a policy per data class **and purpose**, with start event, period/decision rule, legal source, owner, review date, deletion method, exception/hold authority, affected systems and backup policy. Include inputs, rendered/extracted text, source files, restored responses, temporary files, pseudonym mappings, caches, search indexes, reports, logs, consent evidence, rights evidence, processor copies and downstream recipients.
- Proposed states: `active -> restricted | pending_review -> approved_for_deletion -> deleting -> deleted | anonymized`; add `held` with reason/expiry/reviewer, `backup_pending` with access/use suppression and final overwrite date, and `failed` with retry/escalation. Do not mark complete while relevant copies or processor acknowledgments remain unresolved.
- Record deletion batches and per-system results using opaque correlation identifiers, category/policy, timestamps, method, approver, exception and acknowledgment. Do not place raw values, mappings, document excerpts or identity documents in ordinary operational logs. Keep legally necessary case/consent evidence in a separate access-controlled store with its own retention rule; minimization does not mean discarding required evidence.
- Build a restoration guard so recovery from backup reapplies deletion, withdrawal, correction and restriction decisions before restored records resume normal use. A product cannot attest that an independent recipient or user-managed export was deleted without evidence; report that boundary.

Proposed rights states, implementing [sections 30–36](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf), [2026 access rules](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf) and [2024 erasure rules](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf): `received -> verifying -> needs_information | verified -> legal_review -> locating -> actioning -> responded -> closed`, with `partially_fulfilled`, `refused`, `withdrawn`, `overdue`, `processor_pending` and `backup_pending` as explicit outcomes/substates. Configure deadlines by right and source version. Track identity/authority evidence and secure delivery without displaying all source data to intake staff.

| Human legal decision | Automatable support |
|---|---|
| Whether requester/representative has authority; proportional extra evidence | Approved verification methods, limited intake, evidence references, controlled delivery |
| Scope, exceptions, competing rights, partial disclosure, denial reason | Search inventory, propose redactions, route review, preserve decision and notices |
| Purpose necessity, retention law, legal hold and release | Policy calculator, expiry review, scheduled job, deletion retry, copy/processor acknowledgments |
| Whether output is sufficiently anonymous in its context | Evaluate direct/indirect identifiers and re-identification risk, record method and review; never declare anonymity from masking alone |
| Controller/processor roles and lawful instructions | Agreement/register, instruction versions, recipient tasks, incident handoff |
| Transfer location, mechanism and safeguards including accessible recipients | Data-flow/recipient registry, destination/mechanism evidence, review-date gates; do not infer local-only privacy from a local UI |

The table describes implementation responsibility rather than transferring legal judgment to a model. Relevant anchors are [Act sections 6, 20, 28–40](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/17082307.pdf), [erasure clause 6](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/upload/cms/1744195455_4477.pdf), and [access clauses 6–12](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/121423.pdf).

### Gaps

- No complete current inventory of later rights/transfer notifications, destination-specific adequacy decisions, sector-specific laws or organizational role/contract facts was established. The verified 2026 access and 2024 erasure rules materially update the original Act; this is still not a complete legal applicability opinion.
- The exact current section 29 implementing text was located through an official government index; its existence and statutory mechanism are established, but its detailed instrument templates were not fully reviewed. Do not assert a particular SCC/BCR instrument or cloud route is sufficient from these notes.
- The design does not prove implementation, deletion across actual backup/processor systems, access-request handling, or production operation. A narrower supported claim is: “The proposed coverage and workflows are mapped to the identified Thai PDPA provisions and verified notifications, with legal decisions and unverified items explicit.” Avoid “PDPA compliant”, “all sensitive data detected”, and “fully anonymized” marketing claims.
